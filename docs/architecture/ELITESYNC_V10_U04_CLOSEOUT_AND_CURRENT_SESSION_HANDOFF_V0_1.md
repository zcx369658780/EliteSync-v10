## 3. Accepted U-04 product-policy baseline

The accepted baseline preserves:

- a non-collapsible Safety taxonomy;
- case-processing state ≠ person truth;
- user-self protection as distinct from adjudication;
- reversible, minimum-necessary interim protection may precede adjudication but is not guilt, punishment, finding, reputation, or desirability;
- allegation, system observation, evidence, AI interpretation, reviewed finding, restriction, appeal, and unknown remain distinct;
- no automatic `allegation → evidence → finding` promotion;
- distinct review authority classes;
- AI remains advisory only;
- no autonomous finding, allegation validation, emergency truth, moderation outcome, irreversible restriction, or person scoring;
- future Safety-only prioritization/risk assessment remains `NOT DECIDED`;
- report, block, or count patterns must not become guilt, reputation, trustworthiness, bad-actor, Compatibility, Ranking, desirability, or relationship-worth scores;
- Safety evidence MUST NOT flow into ordinary domains;
- ordinary domains may receive only minimum-necessary Safety control directives;
- correction, reversal, or supersession must remove stale ordinary-domain controls and adverse/reputation residue;
- reversal does not imply reporter dishonesty;
- rejected/not-advanced does not imply allegation false;
- consequential reviewed restrictions have a bounded contest/appeal principle under later authority;
- Safety retention requires named purpose, shortest necessity, restricted access, review/expiry, termination, and no ordinary reuse;
- no blanket Safety retention exception;
- `de-identify` and `anonymize` remain distinct;
- external routing remains stopped pending separate legal, factual, product, and operational authority; and
- the nine minimum U-04 semantics are the affected-MVP product-policy gate.

### 3.1 Controlling `actioned/protected` caveat

`actioned/protected` is only a neutral case-processing umbrella. It MUST NOT infer, collapse, or substitute for the distinct underlying authority classes:

1. interim, reversible, minimum-necessary protection; and
2. reviewed, longer-lived, consequential restriction.

Those classes remain semantically distinct. Any later product or technical representation must preserve that distinction even if both may be reflected under the same neutral processing-state umbrella.
