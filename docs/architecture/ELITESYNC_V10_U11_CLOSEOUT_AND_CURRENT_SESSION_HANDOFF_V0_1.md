The following accepted semantics remain controlling.

### 2.1 Distinct responsibility authorities

- Meaning authority, bounded decision authority, execution responsibility, purpose-review authority, lifecycle authority, failure ownership, reconstruction responsibility, and final Owner authority remain distinct.
- Technical custody, storage, visibility, availability, automation, or implementation convenience creates no meaning, purpose, lifecycle, decision, or final product authority.
- No handoff, technical state, AI output, or implementation artifact may silently collapse or transfer these authorities.

### 2.2 Cross-context handoffs and failure responsibility

- Every cross-context handoff preserves source meaning and transfers only minimum-necessary authorized meaning for a named purpose.
- Source-side responsibility owns source validity.
- Receiver-side responsibility owns authorized consumption.
- Disputed boundary authority stops the affected use and routes it to purpose or formal review; authority does not transfer to technical custody, AI, or an adjacent context.
- The ten accepted cross-context interaction contracts remain controlling.
- Missing, stale, contradictory, unauthorized, replayed, or incompletely propagated authority must not create permission.

### 2.3 Connection and communication-access authority

- Relationship owns Connection meaning.
- Matching may present an opportunity but cannot create Connection.
- Valid reciprocal user choice is required for Connection.
- Conversation receives only bounded current communication-access authority.
- Matching, Conversation activity, storage, or technical availability cannot infer Connection, Relationship state, affection, permanence, or person truth.

### 2.4 Named-purpose authority

- Affirmative named-purpose authority is required for cross-context use.
- Storage, visibility, custody, prior use, broad consent, or availability is not sufficient purpose authority.
- Unknown or disputed purpose compatibility stops the specific affected use and routes it to purpose or formal review.

### 2.5 Correction, revocation, reversal, and supersession

Every correction, revocation, reversal, or supersession affecting dependent contexts requires:

1. authoritative source declaration;
2. dependent-consumer stale-effect cleanup; and
3. separate review of whether renewed use is permitted.

Provenance or historical reconstruction must not preserve unauthorized active effect.

### 2.6 Safety-to-ordinary boundary

- Safety-to-ordinary crossing remains minimum-necessary directive only.
- Ordinary contexts receive no Safety evidence, allegation details, reasoning, guilt inference, or person-quality meaning.
- Stale Safety controls must be removed or replaced after authorized correction, reversal, or supersession.
- A Safety control directive does not transfer Safety meaning authority to an ordinary context.

### 2.7 Bounded reconstruction

Consequential cross-context decisions require bounded, minimum-necessary reconstruction. Reconstruction must not transfer authority, disclose unrelated private or Safety information broadly, imply indefinite retention, or preserve unauthorized active effect.

### 2.8 AI and analytics boundary

- AI and analytics remain supporting capabilities only.
- AI or analytics cannot create truth, consent, guilt, Connection, Relationship state, Safety finding, reputation, or new cross-context authority or signals.
- Technical generation, aggregation, prediction, or availability cannot bootstrap a purpose or authority absent from the accepted product-policy contract.

## 3. Controlling scoped fail-closed caveat

`Fail-closed` and `least-permissive` behavior:

- applies only to the specific affected purpose, interaction, and scope whose authority or state is missing, stale, contradictory, unauthorized, or incompletely propagated;
- must not escalate into a global person/account restriction;
- must not become person-level meaning;
- must not imply or become evidence of guilt, wrongdoing, Safety risk, dangerousness, trustworthiness, reputation, bad-actor status, Compatibility, Ranking, desirability, relationship-worth, or any other person truth or character judgment;
- treats bounded denial, omission, or cessation as a safe outcome for the affected scope only; and
- keeps distinct causes and authority classes attributable and prevents their collapse into a generic account state.

The safe outcome is bounded cessation of the unauthorized or uncertain affected use, not a global judgment about the User.
