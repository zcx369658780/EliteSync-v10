# EliteSync 10.0 User Journey and Relationship Domain Language Workshop V0.1

Status: PROPOSED — AWAITING GPT L3 AND OWNER REVIEW

Date: 2026-08-23

## 0. Evidence and decision discipline

### Observed

- Phase 1 proposes that EliteSync help relationship-minded adults allocate limited attention to fewer, better-informed, respectful conversations.
- Phase 1 defines Slow Dating as bounded candidate supply and deliberate attention, not merely delay.
- Phase 1 defines compatibility as a revisable hypothesis and AI as decision support, never the decision maker.
- Competitor screenshots expose self-reflection, identity, matching, content, chat, consultation, and monetization surfaces. They do not establish successful journeys, matching quality, safety outcomes, or retention.

### Inferred

- A relationship journey needs explicit readiness, reciprocal consent, respectful rejection, failure recovery, and user-controlled learning states.
- Treating registration, a score, or a sent message as success would conflict with Phase 1.
- The product needs language that separates a recommendation opportunity from mutual connection and from a human relationship.

### Decision

All decisions below are proposed v10 product-domain decisions. They do not authorize database, API, backend, Flutter, microservice, or code design.

### Unknown

- Direct user research, safety research, legal/compliance review, and quantitative journey benchmarks remain unavailable.
- Phase 1 is committed but still marked as awaiting GPT L3 and Owner review; Phase 2 therefore remains proposed and conditional on that review.

## 1. User lifecycle journey

### Stage 0 — Anonymous Visitor

**User need:** Decide whether EliteSync offers a trustworthy alternative to high-volume browsing, social entertainment, or deterministic compatibility claims.

**Entry promise:** Fewer, explainable introductions; user-owned decisions; no destiny guarantee; clear privacy and cultural-reflection boundaries.

**First value:** A low-risk explanation of the Slow Dating approach and a short reflection that helps the visitor name one relationship priority without requiring a public profile or matching participation.

**Control:** The visitor may leave without creating identity or granting matching consent.

**Exit condition:** The visitor chooses to form an identity because the expected value and data purpose are understandable—not because access is coerced.

**Unknown:** Which first reflection is useful enough without becoming a personality test funnel requires concept testing.

### Stage 1 — Identity Formation

**Meaning:** The user deliberately constructs a self-authored relationship identity from minimum necessary information.

**Concepts involved:**

- basic self-description sufficient for trust and eligibility;
- interests as conversation and lifestyle context;
- values as priorities and trade-offs, not badges;
- personality information as self-reflection, not diagnosis;
- relationship intent, pace, boundaries, and non-negotiables.

**Decision:** Identity formation is progressive, purpose-explained, correctable, and visibility-aware. Completeness is not maximal data collection.

**User value:** The user can see what the product believes it heard and correct it before it affects recommendations.

**Exit condition:** The user has a coherent minimum identity and understands which information is self-declared, interpreted, optional, visible, or used for recommendation.

### Stage 2 — Self Understanding

**Meaning:** The user receives a revisable relationship lens that distinguishes stated facts, interpretations, and unknowns.

**Personality reflection:** Summarizes communication tendencies, needs, and tensions using tentative language and user correction.

**Cultural reflection:** MBTI, astrology, Bazi, or Ziwei may provide opt-in prompts or narratives, visibly labeled as cultural reflection rather than scientific prediction.

**Relationship preference:** Clarifies desired relationship direction, pace, life priorities, boundaries, and acceptable differences.

**First meaningful outcome:** The user can state what matters, what is flexible, and what must be learned from another person rather than “discovering their type.”

**Exit condition:** The user accepts, edits, or dismisses the reflection and chooses whether it may contribute to matching.

### Stage 3 — Match Ready

**Meaning:** A user is Match Ready when participation would be informed, safe enough, and respectful—not merely when registration is complete.

**Decision criteria:**

- relationship intent and current availability are explicit;
- minimum identity is sufficiently coherent to avoid blind recommendation;
- critical boundaries and deal-breakers are represented without requiring exhaustive disclosure;
- the user understands recommendation uncertainty and actively opts in;
- required age, authenticity, consent, and safety eligibility is satisfied at the product-policy level;
- the user can receive, reject, pause, or close introductions respectfully;
- no unresolved safety restriction currently prevents matching.

**Not ready:** Missing or contradictory intent, coerced consent, insufficient minimum context, unresolved eligibility, or a user-requested pause.

**Control:** Readiness is reversible. The user may pause without deleting identity.

### Stage 4 — Curated Match

**Meaning:** The system presents a bounded introduction opportunity supported by a Compatibility Hypothesis. A Match is not a relationship, mutual connection, or compatibility score.

**Why it exists:** Selected signals suggest that a respectful conversation may be worth limited attention, while material uncertainty remains.

**The user sees:**

- the other person's consented, appropriately visible identity context;
- an understandable recommendation rationale;
- areas of possible alignment, possible tension, and missing evidence;
- why cultural reflections, if present, are optional and non-predictive;
- available actions and pacing controls.

**The user may reject:** The introduction, the explanation, a cultural interpretation, specific inferred signals, or further recommendations. Rejection does not imply low human value and must not invite pressure.

**Pacing control:** The user may consider, pass, express interest, pause supply, or close the opportunity. New candidates must not create an effectively infinite queue.

**Transition:** A Match becomes a Connection only when both people independently consent to explore.

### Stage 5 — Conversation

**Start:** A Conversation opens only after reciprocal Connection. Neither one-sided interest nor an algorithm may initiate interpersonal access on behalf of both users.

**Mutual choice:** Either person may decline, delay, end, block, or report. Continued access is not permanent consent.

**AI assistance may:**

- suggest questions grounded in disclosed areas of interest or uncertainty;
- help a user reflect on tone, assumptions, needs, and boundaries;
- summarize only content the user is entitled to use and understands is being processed;
- encourage direct, honest communication and respectful closure.

**AI assistance may not:**

- impersonate either person or send undisclosed messages;
- reveal hidden signals or private interpretations about the other person;
- claim to know feelings or intent;
- manipulate attachment or pressure continuation;
- decide consent, safety, or relationship status.

**Meaningful conversation:** A reciprocal exchange that produces new, user-recognized understanding about fit, difference, intent, boundary, or next step. Message count alone does not qualify.

### Stage 6 — Relationship Development

**Meaning:** The people, not the platform, decide whether their repeated interactions are developing into a relationship.

**Interaction feedback:** Each person may privately reflect on whether communication felt respectful, whether important assumptions changed, and what remains unknown.

**Compatibility update:** New evidence may support, weaken, complicate, or leave unchanged the prior Compatibility Hypothesis. The system must not rewrite disagreement into a higher or lower verdict without explanation and user correction.

**Relationship progress:** Users may describe mutually understood progress, pause, closure, or transition away from matching. The product must not infer an exclusive relationship solely from activity.

**Success boundary:** A respectful decision not to continue can be a successful journey outcome. Continuation is not always superior.

### Stage 7 — Feedback Loop

**Inputs:** Explicit usefulness feedback, corrected identity or preference, user-described interaction learning, respectful closure reasons, and safety outcomes where appropriate.

**Decision:** Feedback improves future explanations, candidate ordering, pacing, or reflection prompts only within the user's understood purpose and control.

**Guardrails:**

- A rejection is not a universal negative label on the rejected person.
- No response is not automatically interpreted as incompatibility.
- Safety reports must not be collapsed into preference feedback.
- Private relationship feedback must not become visible to another user without explicit policy and consent.
- Users can pause learning, correct feedback, request reset, and exercise deletion controls subject to clearly stated safety/legal retention boundaries.

## 2. Relationship domain language

The following vocabulary is proposed as the product-domain language. It defines meaning, not storage or implementation.

### User

- **Meaning:** A person exercising agency in EliteSync, including identity, consent, control, and safety rights.
- **Boundary:** The person remains distinct from every profile, score, model representation, or relationship state.
- **What it is not:** A row, candidate inventory item, engagement target, or fixed algorithmic persona.

### Identity

- **Meaning:** The user's evolving, self-authored account of who they are in the relationship context, including intent and boundaries.
- **Boundary:** Includes declared and user-accepted self-description; inferred interpretations must remain distinguishable.
- **What it is not:** Authentication credentials, a public profile page, a personality type, or a permanent essence.

### Profile

- **Meaning:** A purpose-specific, user-controlled presentation of selected Identity information to a defined audience.
- **Boundary:** Visibility and matching use are separate choices; not all Identity information belongs in a Profile.
- **What it is not:** The whole person, an internal model record, or automatic consent to matching or contact.

### Personality Profile

- **Meaning:** A revisable reflection on user-reported patterns, preferences, or tendencies, with provenance and uncertainty.
- **Boundary:** Cultural frameworks and behavioral evidence remain distinguishable; the user can correct or dismiss interpretations.
- **What it is not:** Clinical diagnosis, scientific certainty, fixed type, destiny, or proof of compatibility.

### Relationship Preference

- **Meaning:** The user's current relationship direction, priorities, desired pace, acceptable differences, and boundaries.
- **Boundary:** It is contextual and changeable; preferences used for recommendation require understood purpose.
- **What it is not:** A guaranteed filter result, entitlement to another person, or a judgment of human worth.

### Compatibility Signal

- **Meaning:** A bounded piece of relevant evidence or declared context that may inform a compatibility question.
- **Boundary:** Every signal has source, purpose, uncertainty, sensitivity, and permitted use; absence is not negative evidence by default.
- **What it is not:** Compatibility itself, a universal trait value, proof of outcome, or hidden permission for sensitive inference.

### Compatibility Hypothesis

- **Meaning:** An explainable, revisable proposition about why two people might or might not find a particular relationship dimension worth exploring.
- **Boundary:** It combines allowed signals with visible uncertainty and is updated by human interaction and correction.
- **What it is not:** A single score, verdict, guarantee, destiny prediction, or statement of either person's value.

### Match

- **Meaning:** A bounded, explainable opportunity offered for each user to consider a potential introduction.
- **Boundary:** It precedes reciprocal consent and may be independently rejected, paused, or allowed to expire.
- **What it is not:** A score, mutual approval, Connection, Conversation, Relationship, or obligation to respond.

### Connection

- **Meaning:** A reciprocal, revocable agreement by two users to explore communication.
- **Boundary:** It grants only the currently explained interaction opportunity and remains subject to consent and safety controls.
- **What it is not:** A Match, friendship, exclusivity, relationship commitment, permanent access, or consent to off-platform contact.

### Conversation

- **Meaning:** A consent-based communication space associated with a Connection.
- **Boundary:** Participation and AI assistance are revocable; access follows block/report and safety decisions.
- **What it is not:** Proof of interest, compatibility, relationship status, therapy, or permission for AI impersonation.

### Interaction

- **Meaning:** A concrete reciprocal or one-sided event through which users may obtain real evidence about communication and fit.
- **Boundary:** Interpretation belongs to context and user reflection; system observation must respect consent and purpose.
- **What it is not:** Automatically positive engagement, an algorithmic outcome label, or proof of a relationship.

### Relationship

- **Meaning:** A human-defined, mutually understood ongoing relational state that may develop through repeated interaction.
- **Boundary:** Users own the meaning and status; the product may support reflection or user-declared state but cannot create it by inference.
- **What it is not:** A Match, Connection, message history, activity threshold, compatibility prediction, or platform-owned lifecycle metric.

### Relationship Feedback

- **Meaning:** A user's contextual account of what an interaction or developing relationship taught them and what should change in their experience.
- **Boundary:** Preference learning, product usefulness, and safety evidence must remain distinguishable and governed by purpose.
- **What it is not:** A public rating of a person's worth, an unappealable reputation score, or objective truth about the other user.

### Ranking

- **Meaning:** A temporary ordering of eligible introduction opportunities for a defined user and purpose.
- **Boundary:** It is constrained by eligibility, consent, safety, pacing, uncertainty, and allowed signals.
- **What it is not:** A hierarchy of human value, attractiveness, virtue, or relationship destiny.

### Recommendation

- **Meaning:** Decision support suggesting that a particular opportunity may deserve attention, with rationale and uncertainty.
- **Boundary:** The user remains free to disagree, reject, pause, or reset.
- **What it is not:** A command, guarantee, expert verdict, or prediction that users belong together.

## 3. Compatibility domain

### Decision flow

```text
Compatibility Signal
↓ permitted, contextual evidence with provenance and uncertainty
Compatibility Hypothesis
↓ explainable reason to explore, not a verdict
Human Interaction
↓ reciprocal real-world evidence and user interpretation
Feedback Update
↓ confirm, contradict, complicate, or leave unknown; revise future support
```

### Rules

- Explicit intent, boundaries, and safety constraints outrank inferred similarity.
- Signals must be relevant to a named dimension; there is no context-free “compatibility.”
- Cultural reflections are labeled and optional; they cannot silently determine a hypothesis.
- Contradictory or missing evidence remains visible rather than being hidden inside a single score.
- Human interaction can update the hypothesis but does not train an immutable judgment about either person.
- Feedback update must preserve source: user correction, interaction learning, product usefulness, and safety report are different meanings.

### Unknown

- The approved signal taxonomy, hypothesis dimensions, evidence strength language, and evaluation method require a dedicated AI Matching and Evidence Workshop.

## 4. Matching language

### Decision

- **Match is not a score:** it is a temporary introduction opportunity.
- **Ranking is not human value:** it orders eligible opportunities for a user-specific, time-specific purpose.
- **Recommendation is not destiny:** it offers a transparent reason to consider, plus uncertainty and control.
- **Mutual interest is not yet a relationship:** it forms a revocable Connection.
- **Conversation activity is not proof of success:** meaningful learning and respectful decisions matter.

The algorithm narrows attention and supplies decision support. It does not grant access, make consent decisions, determine relationship status, or assign personal worth.

## 5. Trust and safety journey

### Rejection

- Either user may decline a Match without supplying a reason to the other person.
- The product acknowledges closure without guilt, repeated pressure, or punitive recommendation behavior.
- Private rejection reasons may improve the rejecting user's experience only within an understood purpose.

### No response

- Silence remains ambiguous and is not labeled as incompatibility, misconduct, or low interest without further evidence.
- The product provides bounded reminders and a respectful expiry/closure path, not escalating emotional pressure.
- Repeated product-level patterns may inform pacing but must not create a public reputation label by default.

### Unmatched or Connection ended

- Either user can end future access.
- The other user receives a neutral state change without private explanation or manipulative prompts.
- Ending a Connection is distinct from blocking and reporting.

### Block

- Blocking immediately prioritizes separation and prevents ordinary contact/discovery as defined by later safety policy.
- It requires no negotiation with the blocked person.
- It is a user protection control, not necessarily a formal allegation or guilt decision.

### Report

- Reporting captures a safety or policy concern through a clear, trauma-aware path.
- Immediate protection, evidence preservation, reviewer access, status communication, appeal, and retention require later policy definition.
- A report is not ordinary preference feedback and must not be processed as matching optimization.

### Harassment

- Users must have immediate exit, block, and report paths without continuing the Conversation.
- AI may identify risk language and offer safety options, but must not make final guilt, emergency, or legal determinations.
- Engagement or prior consent never excuses continued unwanted contact.

### Deception risk

- Users need a way to challenge identity or material representation without conducting surveillance themselves.
- The product may interrupt recommendation or contact when policy thresholds are reached, but later work must define evidence, review, proportionality, appeal, and false-positive handling.
- Compatibility explanations must not imply that identity authenticity has been proven unless a separately defined process supports that claim.

### Unknown

- Safety severity taxonomy, review standards, emergency escalation, evidence access, appeals, identity assurance, repeat-offender policy, and jurisdictional duties require a dedicated Trust, Safety, Privacy, and Compliance Workshop.

## 6. User control and consent

### Decision

The user can:

- inspect and correct the Identity and interpretations used for their experience;
- control which Profile information is visible and for what relationship purpose;
- modify or delete information, subject only to transparent safety/legal retention boundaries;
- opt into or out of cultural reflection and any permitted matching use separately;
- enter, pause, or close recommendation participation;
- reject individual Matches and disagree with rationales;
- reset matching inputs and learned preferences with clear consequences;
- independently consent to, decline, or end a Connection;
- manage a user-declared Relationship state without the system imposing one;
- block and report without requiring reciprocal agreement;
- understand when AI is assisting and refuse its assistance.

Consent is specific, informed, revocable, and not bundled. Profile creation is not matching consent; a Match is not contact consent; a Connection is not permanent consent; Conversation participation is not consent to inference, publicity, or relationship status.

### Unknown

- Exact deletion guarantees, safety retention exceptions, visibility defaults, consent renewal, and recommendation-reset effects require policy and user research.

## 7. Domain boundaries

### Core product domain

- **Identity and Self Understanding:** self-authored relationship identity, reflection, correction, and purpose.
- **Relationship Preference and Readiness:** intent, pace, boundaries, eligibility, and voluntary participation.
- **Compatibility:** allowed signals, hypotheses, explanations, uncertainty, and revision.
- **Matching:** bounded recommendation opportunities and attention pacing.
- **Connection and Conversation:** reciprocal consent, communication context, AI assistance boundary, and closure.
- **Relationship Learning:** user-declared progress, interaction reflection, and feedback update.
- **Trust and Safety Controls:** consent, block, report, immediate protection, and user recourse as product-domain concerns.

### Supporting or peripheral domain

- account access and identity assurance operations;
- moderation operations and case handling;
- notifications;
- payments and subscriptions;
- administration;
- analytics and experimentation;
- customer support;
- legal/compliance operations.

Peripheral does not mean optional or low-risk. It means it supports the relationship value loop rather than defines its central purpose. No technical boundary is selected here.

## 8. MVP journey scope

### MVP supports

- anonymous promise and trust-boundary understanding;
- progressive minimum Identity formation;
- correctable self and relationship reflection;
- explicit Relationship Preference and Match Readiness;
- pause/resume of recommendation participation;
- a small, curated set of explainable Matches;
- independent accept/pass and reciprocal Connection;
- basic consent-based Conversation with clearly bounded optional AI prompts;
- user reflection that confirms, contradicts, complicates, or leaves a Compatibility Hypothesis unknown;
- respectful closure, no-response expiry, unmatch/end Connection, block, and report entry;
- correction, visibility, opt-out, reset, and deletion-request controls at a policy-defined level.

### MVP does not support

- public feeds, followers, live rooms, parties, gifts, virtual currency, or expert marketplaces;
- unlimited browsing or continuous candidate supply;
- group, polyamorous, multi-party, couples-therapy, or family relationship modeling;
- autonomous courtship, AI impersonation, or AI-created relationship status;
- deterministic scoring, public user ratings, or destiny prediction;
- comprehensive historical personality/cultural test inventory;
- complex premium/admin journeys before the core loop is validated;
- emergency, medical, psychological, legal, or abuse intervention beyond safe exit and routing to qualified help;
- database, API, backend, Flutter, microservice, or infrastructure design.

## 9. Open questions for review

- What minimum Identity is sufficient for useful reflection and Match Readiness?
- Which readiness conditions are user-declared, policy-required, or independently assured?
- How should mutual interest expire, pause, or resume without coercion?
- What observable evidence qualifies as a meaningful conversation while respecting message privacy?
- How are one person's private feedback and the other person's rights separated?
- What exact relationship states may users declare, and should MVP support any state beyond exploring, paused, closed, and opted out?
- Which controls produce true deletion versus purpose restriction or safety retention?
- Which Compatibility Signals are permitted, explanation-only, or prohibited?
- How should cultural reflections be labeled and consented to in mixed-belief Matches?
- What safety obligations and escalation pathways apply in the mainland-China launch context?
