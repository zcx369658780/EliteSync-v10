## 1. Why EliteSync 10.0 should exist

### Decision

EliteSync exists to help relationship-minded adults move from ambiguous attraction and overwhelming choice toward a smaller number of better-informed, respectful human conversations.

Its promise is not “find the perfect person.” Its promise is: understand what may matter, see meaningful areas of alignment and tension, choose deliberately, and learn from real interaction without surrendering judgment to an algorithm.

## 2. Target user

### Primary user

Relationship-minded adults in mainland China who:

- want a stable or potentially long-term relationship rather than attention, entertainment, or high-volume chatting;
- feel overwhelmed by shallow profiles, abundant candidates, and pressure to decide quickly;
- are willing to reflect on values, communication style, life direction, boundaries, and cultural context;
- want help forming a sound first hypothesis about compatibility while retaining personal agency;
- can tolerate fewer introductions in exchange for greater context and more intentional attention.

Typical situation: the user can meet or browse people but struggles to decide whom to invest in, what to ask, and how to interpret early differences without overreacting or idealizing.

### Secondary user

- Adults returning to dating after a long relationship who need a structured way to clarify changed priorities.
- Users already talking to someone who want reflection and communication support, not a verdict about the other person.
- Culturally curious users who value MBTI, astrology, Bazi, or Ziwei as reflective language while accepting explicit uncertainty and non-scientific boundaries.

### Non-target user

- Minors.
- Users primarily seeking anonymous entertainment, follower growth, live-room status, gifts, or unlimited casual browsing.
- Users seeking guaranteed destiny prediction, certainty that another person loves them, surveillance, coercive persuasion, or automated control of a relationship.
- Users unwilling to provide truthful minimum context or respect consent and safety boundaries.
- Crisis, abuse, medical, psychological, or legal situations requiring qualified human services rather than dating assistance.

### Unknown

- Exact launch age range, city tier, gender/sexual-orientation scope, relationship-history mix, and inclusion strategy require research and Owner decisions.

## 3. Core problem

### Surface problem

“I cannot find the right person,” “there are too many profiles,” or “I do not know what to say next.”

### Deep problem

Users lack a trustworthy, comprehensible process for deciding where to invest limited relational attention. Current signals are often shallow, fragmented, performative, or falsely precise. Users therefore oscillate between excessive browsing, premature certainty, avoidable rejection, and prolonged ambiguous interaction.

### Decision

EliteSync addresses decision quality under interpersonal uncertainty. It helps users form, test, and revise compatibility hypotheses through reflection and reciprocal human interaction. It does not remove uncertainty or promise a relationship outcome.

## 4. Product position

### Decision

EliteSync occupies the space between self-understanding and human relationship formation:

> From “Who am I?” and “Who is available?” to “Is this conversation worth mutual, respectful attention, and what should we learn next?”

Why it is not Soul:

- Social identity, feeds, public expression, and real-time social presence are not the core value loop.
- EliteSync optimizes considered relational attention, not broad social participation or entertainment inventory.

Why it is not CECE/测测:

- Interpretation of personality or cultural systems is not the end product.
- EliteSync must connect reflection to consent-based interaction, learning, and user-owned decisions; it is not an expert-consultation marketplace or destiny-answer surface.

Why it is not a Tinder-type product:

- Candidate volume, swipe velocity, and immediate visual selection are not the success model.
- EliteSync deliberately limits simultaneous attention and values learning from a small number of meaningful interactions.

### Unknown

- Whether users perceive this position as sufficiently distinct and valuable must be tested with concept interviews and prototype research.

## 5. Slow Dating definition

### Decision

Slow Dating is deliberate allocation of attention under uncertainty. It is not merely a calendar delay.

- **Matching frequency:** introductions arrive in bounded batches or at a deliberately limited cadence. Exact timing is experimental, not frozen as “weekly.” New supply should not outrun the user's ability to consider and respond respectfully.
- **Candidate quantity:** only a small, comprehensible active set is available at once. The product should prevent an effectively infinite queue from becoming the default experience.
- **Attention model:** depth before replacement. Users receive enough context to understand why an introduction may be worth exploring and are encouraged to complete a meaningful consideration or interaction before accumulating more candidates.
- **Choice mechanism:** recommendation narrows attention and explains relevant evidence; the user chooses whether to engage. There is no algorithmic declaration of “the one.”
- **Relationship objective:** discover whether mutual long-term potential deserves further real-world learning, not maximize matches, messages, session time, or emotional dependency.
- **User behavior:** reflect honestly, state intent and boundaries, respond or close respectfully, ask meaningful questions, notice real interaction, and provide feedback about the experience rather than judging a person as a fixed score.

Why: compatibility is partly revealed through reciprocal behavior over time. Unlimited browsing rewards premature comparison and makes every person replaceable before sufficient evidence exists.

### Unknown

- Batch size, cadence, expiration/pausing rules, and respectful closure expectations require user testing and safety review.

## 6. Minimum core value loop

### Decision

```text
User enters with relationship intent, priorities, boundaries, and limited reflective context
↓
Receives first value: a clear self/relationship lens plus one explainable introduction hypothesis
↓
Takes meaningful action: chooses, with mutual consent, to explore or respectfully pass; if exploring, begins a guided but human conversation
↓
Creates feedback: records whether the explanation was useful and what real interaction confirmed, contradicted, or left unknown
↓
Improves future experience: recommendations, explanations, and reflection prompts adapt while preserving user correction and control
```

The loop succeeds when the user makes a better-informed next decision. A match, message, or longer session alone is not success.

### Unknown

- The smallest onboarding input capable of producing useful first value without excessive sensitive-data collection must be tested.

## 7. Compatibility philosophy

### Decision

Compatibility is a revisable, multi-dimensional hypothesis about whether two people may be able and willing to build a mutually acceptable relationship. It includes alignment, manageable differences, reciprocal behavior, timing, intent, safety, and capacity for repair.

An algorithm score:

- compresses selected, incomplete signals for ranking or explanation;
- reflects measurement choices, uncertainty, missing data, and model bias;
- may support attention allocation but cannot observe the future relationship;
- must be decomposable into understandable factors and uncertainty, not presented as objective truth.

Human relationship potential:

- emerges through consent, communication, behavior, context, change, and time;
- cannot be reduced to trait similarity or a single number;
- remains for the people involved to explore and decide.

Therefore the algorithm is decision support, never the decision maker. Users must be able to correct inputs, disagree with explanations, decline recommendations, and provide outcome feedback without being punished for non-compliance.

## 8. AI trust boundary

### AI SHOULD

- summarize user-provided reflection with uncertainty and allow correction;
- explain why a recommendation was made and what evidence is missing;
- surface potential alignment, tension, and questions worth exploring;
- suggest respectful conversation topics and reflection prompts;
- help a user distinguish observation, interpretation, assumption, and need;
- detect when a request exceeds product scope and direct users toward appropriate safety or professional resources;
- learn from explicit feedback within consent, minimization, and user-control boundaries.

### AI SHOULD NOT

- guarantee compatibility, relationship success, love, loyalty, or destiny;
- claim certainty about another person's feelings, motives, mental state, or future behavior;
- manipulate attachment, jealousy, fear, scarcity, urgency, or emotional dependency;
- impersonate a candidate or generate undisclosed messages that misrepresent the user;
- optimize for engagement by withholding, exaggerating, or fabricating relational information;
- replace consent, user judgment, qualified counseling, crisis support, legal advice, or safety decisions;
- use sensitive inferred traits outside an explicitly understood purpose;
- hide material uncertainty or present cultural systems as scientific prediction.

### Decision

Every AI output affecting relational choice must be framed as an explanation or hypothesis, identify uncertainty where material, and preserve a clear human decision point.

### Unknown

- Safety escalation policy, human-review scope, inference taxonomy, data retention, redress, evaluation thresholds, and mainland-China AI compliance require dedicated work.

## 9. Cultural algorithm boundary

### Observed

EliteSync possesses historical MBTI, astrology, Bazi, and Ziwei knowledge. Competitor research shows visible user interest in personality and cultural-interpretation systems. The supplied evidence does not establish predictive validity for relationship outcomes.

### Decision

These systems may serve as opt-in cultural reflection tools: structured languages for self-expression, storytelling, question generation, and discussion of values or perceived patterns.

They must not be presented as scientific proof, deterministic personality diagnosis, fate, or reliable prediction of compatibility. Their outputs must be labeled by source and epistemic status, separated from behavior-based evidence, and correctable or dismissible by users.

MBTI should likewise be treated as a self-reflection vocabulary, not a fixed identity or scientific verdict. Cultural signals must never silently dominate ranking or override explicit user intent, boundaries, safety, or observed behavior.

### Unknown

- Whether any cultural signal belongs in recommendation ranking at all, rather than explanation only, requires evidence, consent design, fairness analysis, and Owner approval.

## 10. MVP non-goals

### Decision

The first product phase will not aim to provide:

- an infinite swipe or high-volume candidate marketplace;
- a public content feed, follower economy, live rooms, group parties, gifting, virtual currency, or expert-consultation marketplace;
- every historical questionnaire, MBTI, astrology, Bazi, Ziwei, scoring, social, profile, and messaging feature;
- deterministic destiny, love, personality, or relationship predictions;
- autonomous relationship decisions or undisclosed automated courtship;
- couples therapy, mental-health diagnosis, crisis intervention, legal advice, or abuse mediation;
- premium/admin feature breadth before the core relationship loop is validated;
- optimization for time spent, message count, match count, or monetization at the expense of decision quality;
- technical platform decomposition, database schema, API design, or legacy parity.

### Unknown

- The minimum messaging boundary, identity verification level, moderation coverage, and monetization experiment remain open; none should be added merely because 9.x or a competitor contains them.

## 11. Proposed product success criteria

### Inferred

The product should ultimately measure whether it improves informed, respectful progression—not only activity.

### Proposed Decision

Phase 2 should define measurable leading indicators for:

- clarity of relationship intent and priorities;
- perceived usefulness and comprehensibility of explanations;
- respectful accept/pass/closure behavior;
- conversion from introduction to mutually chosen meaningful conversation;
- whether real interaction improves or corrects the original hypothesis;
- user agency, safety, correction, and trust.

No numeric target is frozen in this workshop.
